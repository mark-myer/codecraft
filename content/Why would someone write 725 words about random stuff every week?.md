10:25 AM Sunday Evening
It's time to stop fooling myself. My nap this afternoon was long, and I've been lying in bed for half an hour convincing myself that I am sleepy.

I'm not.

It's time to pull out my Macbook and get some relaxation going: writing.

Thoughts bump together in my head, some organized, some random. The best way for me to process them is to put them down on paper (metaphorically speaking).

I once had a friend tell me, "I process life by talking."

I believed him. He can talk the nose off a brass monkey.

That's his gift. Now he's a school teacher, and I'd love to visit his classroom. I'm sure it's great. But that's not quite me. 

Other people process life by talking or writing, each in their own place. I think I'm one of those people. Here's why. 

When you write, you have a much better chance of saying it right. When you're talking, not so much--once the words clear your lips, you can't unsay them. When the words leave your keyboard, you can backspace. Cut. Copy. Paste. Of course, when you send the message or hit *Publish*, it's done. Over. But you had a chance to refine it.

Another advantage of writing: You can store your writings for later. Your present self can talk to your future self. You can see change in your thinking patterns. Progress. (Hopefully).

Writing communicates to a large audience.

*Yeah, but we've got podcasts and audiobooks. This is 2026, by the way.* 

I hear you. Those are great in their place. But would I be recording a podcast at 10:46 on Sunday night? Hardly. Can I write? Sure. Even without electricity or a laptop? No problem. Paper still works. Go figure.

I think the reason a lot of people keep a blog or journal is simply this: Writing helps to process life. And reading other people's writings shows how they are processing life and helps add meaning to yours.

So here's to the CodeCraft blog. If it helps me process life, it is well worth the effort. If it helps anyone else to process their lives, so much the better.

Thanks for reading,

--Marcus
